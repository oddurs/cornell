A surface is a landscape of tiny mirrors, and what it looks like is a statistical question about how they are oriented and how often they hide each other.

Trowbridge and Reitz published the distribution in 1975. Walter rediscovered it in 2007 and the field has called it GGX ever since. The masking term is not a free choice to go with it — Heitz showed in 2014 that it follows from the distribution by requiring the microsurface to project to the macrosurface, and here it is derived rather than looked up.

The furnace test from v0.5 will fail, honestly and visibly, and then be made to pass.
