## What it has to explain

That the colour matching functions are not a law. They are an average of a
small number of measurements made on a small number of young men in London
around 1930, they have known inaccuracies in the blue, and they are the
foundation of a hundred-billion-dollar display industry.

House rule 8: the credit is also a warning, and this is the most load-bearing
example of it in the project.

## What it must not do

Sneer. Wright and Guild did extraordinary work with the equipment they had,
and the functions have survived a century of scrutiny.

## 2026-09-14

cie.hpp carries the full argument - Wright's ten and Guild's seven, the blue problem, Judd and Vos, the 2-degree caveat, and the refusal to sneer. What was missing was any mention of it where a reader arrives, so the README has a section now, placed before the box rather than after it: the observer is the one input to this project that measures people rather than the world, and that belongs next to the two claims rather than buried in a header.
